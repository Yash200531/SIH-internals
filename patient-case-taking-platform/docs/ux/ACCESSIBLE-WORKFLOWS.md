# Accessible and Low-Literacy Workflows

## Patient experience

- Voice-first, but always provide text and assisted alternatives.
- Use one primary action per screen and large touch targets.
- Pair icons with spoken and written labels.
- Allow language switching without losing progress.
- Provide repeat, slow-down, skip and “I don't know” actions.
- Read captured information back before confirmation.
- Make emergency escalation visually and audibly distinct.
- Display explicit offline, syncing and saved states.
- Support caregiver/delegate mode without impersonating the patient.

## Clinician experience

- Show a chronological timeline rather than a wall of generated prose.
- Prioritize allergies, medications, red flags and unresolved questions.
- Link each generated statement to its source utterance/document.
- Highlight AI uncertainty without forcing alert fatigue.
- Preserve the clinician's edit and signature as the authoritative note.

## Evaluation

Test with intended users in realistic noisy environments. Measure task completion, comprehension, corrections, abandonment, assistance required and time-on-task—not only satisfaction.
